Postal route of one cardHow it works

You log a QSO

Press Log QSO in WSJT-X as usual. FT8, FT4, JT65, whatever you run.

via UDP · port 2237

Card is queued

A delay you choose (30 min by default) lets you fix or delete the QSO first.

one card per call + band + mode

E-mail lookup

The other station's address is found on QRZ.com, with HamQTH as a fallback.

QRZ XML · HamQTH XML

Card is drawn

Your design, their callsign, date, UTC, band, mode and report, rendered pixel-exact.

JPEG 1400 × 900 or your size

Sent from your mail

Gmail with an app password, or any SMTP server. Each card lands in the log with a copy.

SMTP · SSL/TLS

Back of the cardFeatures

This card confirms

  • WSJT-X native. Listens to the UDP protocol: heartbeat, status, logged ADIF. Multicast works with GridTracker and JTAlert on the same port.
  • Visual card editor. Text, QSO panel and image layers on top of your own photo. Fonts included: Bebas Neue, Montserrat, Oswald, Caveat and more.
  • A gallery of designs. Keep several cards enabled and each QSO gets one at random, or pin a single design.
  • Honest rendering. The editor and the sender share one render routine, so the preview is the card.
  • Lookup that falls back. QRZ.com XML first, HamQTH second, or the other way round.
  • Rules you control. Send delay, cards per 24 h, blocked prefixes, retries, deduplication per call, band and mode.
  • Your e-mail text. Subject and body templates with variables, including your postal address for a paper QSL in return.
  • Passwords stay home. Encrypted locally with AES-256-GCM; the OS keychain is optional.
  • Five languages. English, Ukrainian, Russian, German, Spanish.
  • Imports the old script. One click reads config.ini, the QSO log and your background picture.

For the old-school operatorThe Python script

autoqsl.py

Terminal · Python 3 · Pillow · requests
Prefer a window with scrolling text and a config.ini? The original script does the same job in 500 lines: listens to WSJT-X, looks up the e-mail, draws the card on your photo and sends it through Gmail. The desktop app can import everything from it later. 
$ python3 autoqsl.py                  # run, listen to WSJT-X
$ python3 autoqsl.py --preview UR5XXX  # draw a sample card -> preview.png
$ python3 autoqsl.py --test-email      # send a sample card to yourself
$ python3 autoqsl.py --status          # show the queue / sent log
$ python3 autoqsl.py --lookup N0CALL   # which e-mail would be used
On a Mac, double-click start_autoqsl.command and it sets up its own Python environment on first run.
